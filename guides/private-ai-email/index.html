Private AI email: what stays on your Mac

Short answer: An AI email app is only as private as the place its AI runs. Most AI mail apps send your messages to their own cloud or to a model provider. The most private setup keeps mail between you and your provider, runs AI on your Mac, and only uses a cloud model you chose, when you ask. Margin, a native Mac email app for Gmail, is built that way, and its AI never sends.

There are five common setups. Ask any mail app which one it uses.

None of these is wrong. They’re trade-offs between capability, convenience and how many companies handle your mail.

Five questions to ask any AI email app

  1. Does the app maker run a server that stores or processes my mail? If yes, ask what’s kept and for how long.
  2. Which AI model reads my mail, and under whose account? Yours, the app maker’s, or your mail provider’s?
  3. Does AI run when I’m not asking? Background triage means more of your mail is read by a model.
  4. Can the AI send email, or change things, on its own? Look for “drafts only” and an explicit send step.
  5. Does the app work with AI off? If not, privacy and usefulness are tied together.

What’s true in every tier:

  • No Margin server. Margin talks to Google directly from your Mac. Its own small settings live in your Google Drive’s hidden app folder.
  • No analytics. We can’t see how you use Margin.
  • AI never sends. Write it fills the composer; you read it and press ⌘⏎. Anything the draft added that the thread didn’t have (a new recipient, a link, a number or a code) is flagged.
  • Tier 3 is click-only and read-only. Your Claude runs with Margin’s read-only tools; it can’t send, delete, create filters or fetch the web.
  • Off means off. Turn AI off in Settings › Intelligence and every rules feature keeps working.

Who should choose what

  • Want the most powerful AI and don’t mind a vendor cloud? A vendor-cloud app gives you more automation today.
  • Already pay for Gemini or Copilot? Your provider’s AI keeps mail inside the company that already hosts it.
  • Want AI with no third party at all? Apple Mail’s on-device features, or Margin’s Tier 2, keep it on your device.
  • Want strong AI drafting, but under your own account? Bring-your-own-model setups like Margin’s Tier 3 or Missive’s API keys.
  • Want no AI? Mimestream, or Margin with AI off.

FAQ

What is the most private AI email app for Mac?

The most private setup has no app-maker server, runs AI on your Mac, and only uses a cloud model you chose, when you ask. Margin is built that way for Gmail: rules and Apple Intelligence on your Mac first, your own Claude only on click, and AI never sends.

Is there an email client that doesn’t store my emails on its servers?

Yes. Some native clients connect directly to your mail provider from your device, with no sync server of their own. For Gmail on the Mac, Margin and Mimestream both work this way; Margin also runs no analytics and keeps its AI on your Mac or your own Claude.

Does on-device AI mean my email never leaves my Mac?

For the on-device part, yes: the model runs locally and nothing is uploaded. Margin’s Request notes use Apple’s on-device model. Apple Mail also uses Private Cloud Compute for some requests. If you opt into a cloud model, like your own Claude, the conversation you send goes to that provider.

Can I use my own Claude with Gmail?

Yes, with Margin on a Mac. Margin finds the Claude Code you installed and uses it, under your own plan, only when you click Write it or What’s been decided. It never sees your Claude login and doesn’t use an API key.

Can AI email apps send messages without asking?

Some can. Several apps let connected agents send mail, sometimes without approving each message. Margin’s AI can only draft; sending is always your keystroke.
